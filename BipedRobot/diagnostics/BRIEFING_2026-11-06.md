# Biped gait problem: briefing, 6 Nov 2026

## Goal

A 12-joint hobby-servo biped must walk in a straight line with a clean alternating step: lift one leg, place it, lift the other, place it, at about 2 steps per second, with an upright torso. Speed does not matter (0.05–0.15 m/s is fine). The policy is trained in simulation and must run on the real robot.

## Setup

**Robot**
- AsterisCrack BipedRobot V2 design, 1.66 kg, 6 joints per leg: hip yaw, hip pitch, hip roll, knee, ankle pitch, ankle roll.
- Feetech STS3215 servos at 12 V, PLA feet with a 9 cm sole.
- A Raspberry Pi runs the policy at 50 Hz, with an IMU (gyro and gravity).

**Simulator (MuJoCo)**
- 5 ms physics step, 10 s episodes.
- Position actuators with a servo torque-speed limit (2.94 N·m cap, 5.3 rad/s no-load), plus delay, lag and backlash fitted to the real servos (below).
- Randomized:
  - floor friction 0.25–0.8;
  - mass ±20%;
  - motor strength ±15%;
  - sensor noise and per-episode bias;
  - encoder rounding (4096 steps);
  - joint speed computed from angle differences, as on the robot.

**Learning: SAC with hindsight relabeling (HER), off-policy, 8 parallel envs, 7.2M steps (about 4 h per run)**
- The goal is a velocity command [vx, vy, wz] plus three "token budgets" for flight, slip and torque.
- Hindsight relabeling replaces the goal with velocities the robot actually achieved over a window.
- The critic sees privileged simulator state; left-right mirror augmentation is on.
- Actor inputs, all things the robot can measure:
  - 5 frames (0.1 s) of joint angles, joint speeds, gyro, gravity and the previous action;
  - the command and budgets;
  - optionally a gait clock (sin and cos) and a slow yaw-rate input.
- Action: joint-target offsets through a low-pass filter (alpha 0.3).

**Reward**
- Tracking kernel: exp(−|v − v_cmd|² / w_v²) · exp(−(w − w_cmd)² / w_w²), with widths that scale with the command.
- Alive bonus 2.5, fall penalty 25.
- Gait shaping:
  - touchdown air time 3.0, single support 0.05, flight −0.5, zero-touchdown −0.3, progress 0.3;
  - optional: "feet on the clock" (phase_weight × (number of feet matching the clock − 1)), swing height on the clock, edge-stance penalty, foot-drag penalty.

## Real robot (the owner's ground test)

A policy was run on the ground. It was most likely the plain policy below, the one exported for the robot, but the owner has not said which:
- it made the walking motions;
- it walked on its toes and took far too many steps;
- it tilted forward and fell without support;
- it did not walk straight.

Not yet known: exactly which checkpoint was used, whether the servos' acceleration cap was cleared, and whether the driver's torque cap was 50% or 100%.

## What has been measured (simulator, proven)

**Reward-free policy (7.2M steps, 5 seeds)**
- Meets the original survival and speed goal on all 5 seeds.
- Its gait:
  - about 6 short steps per second, swing lift 8–12 mm;
  - standing on the sole's edge 70–88% of loaded time;
  - torso pitched 5.5–6.6° forward, centre of mass 40 mm ahead of the feet's midpoint (5 mm when standing);
  - turning 0.19–0.45 rad per 10 s.

**Attempts at a human-like gait (2 seeds each, "lo–hi" = range over seeds and budgets):**

**Toe walking.** How each foot touches the floor while it carries weight (mid budget, command 0.2):

| Policy | Whole sole down | Toe | Heel | Edge | Median sole angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain (the one run on the robot) | 1% | 28–32% | 0–1% | 65–71% | 14–19° |
| Designer-style | 6–8% | 57–58% | 19–25% | 12–14% | 7–11° |
| Heel-toe | 9–18% | 47–48% | 15% | 20–30% | 5–7° |

So the simulator shows the toe walking the robot showed. The designer-style penalty counts only sideways (edge) tilt, so toe stance costs nothing.

**Swing-foot taps.** Contact mapped against the gait clock shows that both shaped arms keep the clock's 2 steps/s rhythm for their main stances. But the swing foot is on the floor 10–30% of its swing time. Every tap of 60 ms or more counts as a step, which explains the high step and chatter counts.

**Slower command doesn't help.** At command 0.1 m/s instead of 0.2, the same policies still take 3.6–6 steps/s, chatter 1.2–4.2/s and drift 0.2–0.85 m sideways.

**Heading hold.** A gyro-only heading hold at test time (yaw command = −k · integrated yaw, no retraining):
- designer-style arm: turn 0.9 → 0.3 rad, sideways 1.0 → 0.3–0.55 m, survival down a few points;
- heel-toe arm: barely changed, and one seed fell more often (96% → 80% at the mid budget).

**Real servos.**
- Each STS3215 ships with a hidden acceleration cap of about 8 rad/s². It has been cleared on the robot.
- Cap cleared, no acceleration ramp, unloaded:
  - step response t63 51–74 ms;
  - 5° sine: gain 0.98 at 1 Hz, 0.94 at 2 Hz, 0.81 at 3 Hz, about 55 ms lag.
- Simulator fit: delay 0–20 ms, lag 10 ms, backlash 0.2°.
- The robot's driver config caps torque at 50% (the training assumes 100%).

## Why it fails (diagnosis: hypotheses based on reading the code and the contact map)

1. **The policy can't sense a slow turn.** It sees 0.1 s of gyro, and the gyro swings about ±1 rad/s with every step (rms 0.8–1.1). A 0.09 rad/s curve is invisible.
2. **The reward barely penalizes curving.** The yaw kernel was 0.15 rad/s wide over 0.5 s windows that the step swing dominates. A 0.09 rad/s curve keeps 70% of the tracking reward. Nothing scores heading or sideways position over the episode.
3. **Swing-foot taps are cheap.** phase_weight 0.1 makes a mid-swing touchdown cost 0.05 per step, far less than the alive bonus a tap protects. So the policy uses taps for balance.
4. **Speed dominated the reward.** Commands were 0.1–0.3 m/s.
6. **Toe stance was free.** No reward-free policy has any flat-foot term. The shaped ones penalize only sideways tilt, because toe-off was allowed for 0.2 m/s. An earlier check on the model found a whole-sole walk at 2 steps/s reaches only about 0.1 m/s; 0.2 m/s needs 2.5–2.9 steps/s and joint speeds beyond the servos. Standing on the toes moves the support point forward, which fits the 26–41 mm forward centre of mass and the forward fall.
5. **Hindsight relabeling may weaken the pressure to go straight.** A curved walk gets relabeled as a perfect "curve" episode. Not tested.

Unknowns:
- why the designer-style arm always turns left;
- the servos' behaviour under load;
- how well the contact model matches real PLA feet;
- how much of the robot's forward fall comes from the gait itself, and how much from half torque or capped servo acceleration during the test.

## Running now (results about 00:45, 7 Nov)

"Straight step", 4 seeds. It is the designer-style arm plus the fitted servo model, with these changes:
- clock 1.0 Hz (2 steps/s);
- tracking window 1.0 s, one full gait cycle, so the step swing cancels;
- yaw kernel width 0.10 rad/s;
- a new input: the mean gyro yaw rate over 1 s;
- phase_weight 0.5;
- swing height 30 mm at weight 0.6;
- commands vx 0.05–0.15.

All changes go in at once. Pass lines were set beforehand: the targets in the table above, with the heading hold on, at command 0.1.

After the owner's ground-test report, seeds 5 and 6 were stopped and replaced by a paired one-change variant, "flat foot" (seeds 3 and 4). It is the same as straight step except the flat-foot penalty counts the sole's whole angle, so toe and heel stances pay too. Extra pass line: whole sole down at least 80% of loaded time. With 2 seeds per arm, any pass is an early sign only. Results about 01:05.

## Constraints on any proposal

- **Compute:** at most 4 training runs at once on one shared GPU. A 7.2M-step run takes about 4 h; no resume.
- **Code:** the core environment, trainer and evaluation files are frozen. Changes must be new config options (off by default) or new configs in the project's own package.
- **Policy inputs:** only what the robot measures (joint angles, angle-difference speeds, gyro, gravity, a clock, the command). The critic may use simulator truth.
- **Servo limits:** about 5.3 rad/s no-load, 2.94 N·m cap.
- **Reporting:** results with gait rewards or a gait pattern are reported apart from reward-free results. Claims need the seeds fixed in advance and fresh test seeds.
- **Out of scope:** servo heating models, a separate price head, MJX/Playground/Warp.
