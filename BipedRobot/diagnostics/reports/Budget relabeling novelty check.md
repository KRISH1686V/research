# Only the rolling-budget combination survives scrutiny

**No paper found breaks the full claim. But most of its parts are already published, so only a narrow combination can be claimed as new.** Relabeling a budget in hindsight is taken. SaFormer (2023) resets a cost-limit token to the realized torque cost during online fine-tuning on simulated locomotion ([SaFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12203)). SeqRoute (May 2026) already uses the name **"Hindsight Budget Relabeling"** and replays logs exactly under virtual budgets ([SeqRoute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25424)). A budget as a policy input is taken, including on a real quadruped ([QuietPaw](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05035v1)). Velocity-command hindsight relabeling on a legged robot is taken ([CHRL](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11564515/)). The link between multipliers and the slope of the value is textbook ([Boyd & Vandenberghe](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf)). **What survives is the combination.** That means a rolling (capped, refilling) budget, relabeled with every bucket level recomputed along the stored trajectory, jointly with the velocity command, in online off-policy training, for several gait limits at once. A narrow price claim also survives: no paper found tests prices read from a budget-conditioned deep critic's gradient against trained multipliers. That core can carry a Q1 journal paper only if the paper shows it is more than "SeqRoute with a token bucket" or "CHRL plus QuietPaw", and shows that the budget input really changes the gait. Every "not found" below is a search result from arXiv, OpenAlex and web search. Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar were unavailable, and several flagged papers remain unread. This report judges novelty only. It does not judge readiness.

## The claim has seven parts, and each close paper misses most of them

Plain definitions first. A **total budget** is one allowance per episode that only goes down. A **rolling budget** either refills at a fixed rate up to a cap (a token bucket) or counts cost over the last T steps (a sliding window). Both limit cost inside any time window, not just per episode. **Hindsight relabeling** means rewriting the budget stored with old data, so the same data trains the policy for other budgets. **Joint relabeling** means rewriting the budget and the velocity command together. **Online off-policy** means the robot keeps collecting data while a SAC-style learner reuses old data from a replay buffer. **Offline** means learning only from a fixed log. A **price readout** means reading, from the critic's slope, how much reward one more unit of budget buys.

Two patterns stand out. **Every budget-relabeling paper uses a total budget, and all except SaFormer and CHER are offline.** The only paper with the exact rolling recursion, Qiu et al., updates all bucket levels for one fixed bucket setting and never relabels across limits ([Qiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00485)). The legged papers have hardware and online training, but none relabels a budget.

## Six earlier papers already relabel budgets in hindsight

### Total budgets have been relabeled since 2019

CHER is the earliest paper found that names a hindsight mechanism for constraints. For each stored transition, it also trains on every other constraint target in a finite set. It does this inside an off-policy loop on replayed ad logs ([CHER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03149)). Its constraint is a per-request ratio, though. No balance builds up over time.

**SaFormer is the most dangerous paper for the claim.** Its online fine-tuning loop rolls out a trajectory, recomputes cost-to-go along it, and resets the cost-limit token. In its words, "we use the actual cost return as the value of token D when we relabel the newly collected trajectory" ([SaFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12203)). Its cost is total joint torque, chosen "to prolong the lifespan of motors", on simulated Hopper, Walker2d and HalfCheetah ([SaFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12203)). So a torque budget, relabeled in hindsight, on a locomotion task, with an online phase, already exists. SaFormer lacks a rolling budget, a command, a set of gait limits, and hardware.

**SeqRoute owns the name.** "Inspired by Hindsight Experience Replay, HBR retrospectively simulates recorded trajectories under a spectrum of hypothetical initial budgets." It replays the same actions through the exact budget transition, ends the episode when the budget runs out, and sweeps five virtual budgets ([SeqRoute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25424)). It argues this is valid because "the budget transition is a bookkeeping operation". The claim needs the same argument. SeqRoute is offline, uses a per-session total, has no command, and routes LLM queries.

The rest fill in the picture. CDT relabels cost and return targets onto safe trajectories, offline ([CDT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07351)). HALO replaces an ad budget with the realized cost and calls this "the Hindsight Mechanism" ([HALO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03267)). BCRL samples a fresh budget onto each logged transition and calls it "data augmentation" ([BCRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22292)). GAS resamples segment cost targets ([GAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05323)). All are offline, and all use totals.

**Exact recomputation of the balance is therefore not new for total budgets.** SeqRoute replays an exact transition. SaFormer and HALO recompute cost-to-go from realized costs.

**There is also a framing risk.** Prior work relabels in three styles. One sweeps virtual budgets (SeqRoute, BCRL, CDT, CHER). One substitutes the achieved cost (SaFormer, HALO). One perturbs targets (GAS). The phrase "budget as a hindsight goal" reads like the achieved-cost style, which is exactly SaFormer and HALO. The paper must say which style it uses. A 2026 paper also warns that a cost "is never reached so much as minimized", so a cost lacks the natural "achieved" value that a goal has ([Coverage collapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26918)). A reviewer can use that against the "goal" framing.

### The token bucket and the window bound are old; relabeling them was not found

Qiu et al. use the identical recursion, n[t+1] = min(b, n[t] − a[t] + r), with a DQN ([Qiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00485)). They update "Q-values for all token bucket states at the same time without resampling training data", which they describe as a way to "avoid doing proper reinforcement learning". It works because "our inputs (and metrics) are statistically independent of actions". Their training data is generated "Given a token bucket configuration", and the limits r and b are not policy inputs.

Chakrabarti et al. used the same recursion with value iteration, not RL ([Chakrabarti et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.13737v2)). The bound "cost over any T steps is at most r·T + B" is classical network calculus ([Le Boudec & Thiran](https://leboudec.github.io/netcal/latex/netCalBook.pdf)). The same recursion is a virtual queue with the sign flipped, as in a 2025 DQN paper ([Zakeri et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01406)). A networking reviewer will see this at once. An "anytime" constraint paper allows refueling through negative costs, but without a cap it bounds only sums from the episode start, not every window ([McMahan & Zhu](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/mcmahan24a/mcmahan24a.pdf)). **The cap is what makes a budget rolling.**

Sliding windows exist too, but without limit inputs or relabeling. A bandit paper enforces every realized window exactly ([Sliding-window bandits](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08745)). An auto-bidding paper bounds a ratio over every 7-episode window, but its controller cannot see the window state ([Sliding-window bidding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25233)). SDT turns per-step cost into a 5-step windowed cost for one fixed rule, offline ([SDT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17217)). SDGD conditions one planner on sub-trajectory limits, but it has no refill and does not relabel the limit ([SDGD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02777)). The nearest locomotion-side relative is a fatigue model with a recovering state, trained on one setting and tested on many ([Cheema et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08583)).

Two conceptual links matter. Reward-machine methods already recompute an auxiliary tracker along stored data to make counterfactual experience ([CRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03950); [LCER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02135)). A token bucket is a tracker of that kind. Separately, Sauté's authors suggest "exploiting the known safety state transitions" to recover efficiency ([Sauté](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06558)). Reading that as relabeling is an inference, but a reviewer may read it that way.

**What is left, by the researchers' reasoning, is path dependence.** A total budget has one crossing point per budget. With a cap and a refill, the same cost trace produces a different level history for each (r, B), including credit lost while the bucket is full. Changing the budget changes every later level and every violation flag. No paper found does this recomputation across limits. This argument is an inference, not a statement in any paper.

## Legged work relabels commands and conditions on limits, but never relabels budgets

**CHRL is the closest legged paper.** It relabels the velocity command in an off-policy replay buffer. It samples a new command within ±0.3 m/s and ±0.5 rad of the original and recomputes rewards from stored robot state ([CHRL](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11564515/)). It trains with REDQ in PyBullet and deploys zero-shot on a 15 kg quadruped with 12 actuated joints. Foot slip is a penalty ramped by a curriculum, not a budget. These details came through a summarizing fetch tool and need a hand check.

**QuietPaw gives a legged policy a cost threshold as input** and lets the operator change it at run time: "the ϵ acts as a noise reduction effect steering command" ([QuietPaw](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05035v1)). It runs PPO-Lagrangian on a real Unitree Go2, with one threshold and one trained multiplier per simulated environment. Its single cost mixes contact force and impact velocity. There is no flight, slip or torque limit, and nothing is relabeled. **QuietPaw also carries the sharpest warning for the claim:** "Simply concatenating the noise constraint into the policy input, often fails to induce meaningful behavioral differences" ([QuietPaw](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05035v1)). It also starts with a gait reward and decays it.

GentleHumanoid feeds a force limit as an observation on a real G1 and resamples it every 5 seconds. It enforces the limit with a clamp and a penalty, and the limit concerns arm contact, not gait ([GentleHumanoid](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04679v1)).

Joint relabeling of a goal with a second quantity exists outside legged robots. TDM relabels goal and time horizon together, online and off-policy, with a real Sawyer arm test ([TDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09081)). RADT relabels goals and avoid regions together, offline ([RADT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19337)). UDRL builds return-plus-horizon commands in hindsight ([UDRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02877)). Generalized Hindsight relabels a task vector that holds a goal plus penalty weights for obstacles and action size, with SAC ([Generalized Hindsight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11708)). A reviewer could call that "goal plus soft-constraint relabeling". The paper should explain that weights are not limits. LP3 relabels multiplier-like preference weights on replay samples in simulated walker and humanoid tasks with an energy constraint. Its thresholds stay fixed, and it "has not been validated yet on a real robot platform" ([LP3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/huang22a.html)). Hindsight Preference Replay relabels preference weights in a SAC-based buffer, including on a simulated biped walker, with no constraints ([HPR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11604)).

Legged constraint work keeps its limits fixed. CaT uses fixed air-time, contact-count and torque constraints ([CaT](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18765)). On the low-cost Bolt biped, "Switching between walking and jumping behaviors requires retraining the network" ([Bolt with CaT](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02194)). ECO searches energy thresholds on a humanoid by retraining at each one, and "Each threshold search takes around 4 hours of wall-clock time" ([ECO](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06445)). That is the cost the claim removes, so ECO is a good motivating citation.

Three side parts are not new. **Training without gait rewards** is established ([LoComposition](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15896); [Not Only Rewards](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12517); [Fu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.01674)). LoComposition even reports that air-time and contact-count constraints hurt its quadruped, a counterpoint reviewers may raise. **Off-policy training on a low-cost small humanoid** exists on the ROBOTIS OP3 with Dynamixel servos, with MPO ([Haarnoja et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.13653)) and with SAC ([Masuda & Takahashi](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.03897v4)). **SAC with symmetry augmentation** on humanoids is in FastSAC ([FastSAC](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01996)). The low-cost servo bipeds found, such as ToddlerBot and Open Duck Mini, train with PPO ([ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893); [Open Duck runner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground/main/playground/common/runner.py)). No SAC biped on Feetech-class hobby servos was found. That is a platform fact, not a method contribution, and it counts only if the paper includes hardware results. **No budget-relabeling paper found used a real robot.** Hindsight relabeling has reached real robots only for commands, goals or quality labels: CHRL, TDM, RbSL ([RbSL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01734)) and QCP ([QCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37393)).

## Critic-read prices rest on textbook theory and three near-precedents

**The theory cannot be claimed.** Where the optimal value is differentiable, each multiplier equals minus the slope of the value with respect to its constraint ([Boyd & Vandenberghe, eq. 5.58](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf)). For constrained MDPs, the value is concave in the constraint relaxation, and every multiplier is a subgradient of it ([Paternain et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.13393); [Ding, Huan & Ribeiro, Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.17194)). 2026 papers state the identity in passing as "a standard consequence of the envelope theorem" ([CLAUSE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21035)). One calls the multiplier "the shadow price of the average-power budget" ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25680)).

Three papers come close to the readout itself. Boutilier & Lu compute "bang-per-buck" slopes from a tabular budget-conditioned value and allocate budget by them ([Boutilier & Lu](https://www.auai.org/uai2016/proceedings/papers/246.pdf)). RLB fits a neural network to the value difference V(t,b+1) − V(t,b) and bids with it ([RLB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.02490)). BFTQ trains a deep budget-conditioned critic, and "a single training of BFTQ corresponds to 10 trainings of FTQ(λ)" ([BFTQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01004)). None of the three compares a read price with a trained multiplier. CCPO is a useful contrast: its critics take the threshold as input, yet it still solves for the multipliers separately ([CCPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03718)). Revenue management has long defined a bid price as "the marginal value of capacity" ([Bid-price paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07391)). Operations-research reviewers will see the readout as a transfer of an old idea.

**No published comparison of a critic-read price with trained multipliers was found.** The nearest numbers show that trained multipliers are weak references. In a small tabular problem, a trained Lagrangian multiplier settled **16.7% above** the exact price ([Rawat 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08956)). Deep RL multipliers oscillate during training ([Stooke et al.](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/stooke20a/stooke20a.pdf)). One study describes the slope of a learned cost-return curve only in words, without numbers ([Spoor et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17564)).

The risks are specific to rolling budgets. The concavity results assume budgets met in expectation, with the policy free to allocate future budget ([Boutilier & Lu](https://www.auai.org/uai2016/proceedings/papers/246.pdf); [BFTQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01004)). A token bucket fixes the next level by physics. From this, the researchers infer that V(s,b) need not be concave, so the theorems do not cover it. They also infer that the price is zero whenever the cap binds, that it drifts by 1/γ per step under a discounted reward, and that SAC's entropy weight must match between the two estimators. RLB gives direct evidence of noise: the learned difference "fluctuates heavily when b is very small" ([RLB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.02490)). A defensible test uses three references: an exact answer on a small constrained MDP, finite-difference retrains at b ± δ, and SAC-Lagrangian multipliers. It should include a limit that does not bind, where the price should be near zero. The project's own rule of five seeds before any big claim applies here.

**No paper found breaks the full claim.** Several break single fragments, as the table shows. SaFormer comes closest on mechanism. SeqRoute comes closest on name and exact replay. Qiu comes closest on the bucket. QuietPaw comes closest on a budget-conditioned legged policy, and CHRL on command relabeling for legs.

Here is a wording the search supports, with "to our knowledge" and the search scope stated: *we relabel rolling (token-bucket) gait budgets in hindsight, recomputing every bucket level and violation flag exactly along the stored trajectory, jointly with the velocity command, during online off-policy actor-critic training; one run thereby conditions a single legged policy on flight, stance-slip and torque-saturation budgets.* A second, narrower contribution: *we test marginal budget prices read from the budget-conditioned critic's gradient against trained multipliers and finite-difference retrains, under rolling budgets.*

**Is that enough for a Q1 journal? On novelty, yes in this narrowed form, by this search.** But the novelty is thin, so the evidence has to carry it. Reviewers will say "swap SeqRoute's recursion for a token bucket", "add (r, B) to Qiu's input" or "combine CHRL and QuietPaw". The paper needs four answers. First, it must explain why path-dependent recomputation matters, and why Qiu's shortcut fails on a robot, where actions change the next state (both are inferences from the notes). Second, it must show the budget input really changes the gait, given QuietPaw's warning. Third, it must show that relabeling beats sampling one budget per environment, as QuietPaw does, and that one run matches per-limit retrains like ECO's. Fourth, it must state the bound honestly. The any-window guarantee r·T + B holds only if the bucket never goes below zero. With a soft penalty, the bound is conditional (inferred from summing the recursion over a window). Do not use the name "Hindsight Budget Relabeling" without citing SeqRoute.

## Conclusion

The search moves the paper's center of gravity. "Budgets as hindsight goals" is not the contribution, because SaFormer and SeqRoute already did that for totals. The contribution is one mechanism: exact, path-dependent recomputation of a capped, refilling budget across many limits, inside an online off-policy loop, on a legged robot. The most direct way to turn that into a reviewer-proof result, by the researchers' reasoning, is an ablation. Run the same pipeline with a total budget relabeled SeqRoute-style, then show what only the rolling version delivers, such as burst control inside short windows. If the two behave the same, the novelty collapses into the prior work. If they differ, the difference is the paper.

Two forward-looking points follow. First, the field is converging quickly. GAS, BCRL, SDGD, SeqRoute, the window-bidding paper, the window-bandit paper and QCP all appeared between February and early November 2026. The risk of being scooped is real, and the late re-search in row 5 is not optional. Second, the price readout is the least crowded part but the most exposed in theory. No source shows that the value under a physical token bucket is concave. A proof, or even a clean empirical concavity check on a small exact problem, would fill a gap the search found. That would be a stronger contribution than the readout alone.
